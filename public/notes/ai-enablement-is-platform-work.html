A surprising amount of AI enablement is platform engineering

November 2026 · 3 min read

Over the past year I've helped teams across customer experience move a portfolio of AI ideas toward real use. The pattern I keep seeing is that the model is rarely the hardest part. What stops most projects is everything around it.

A promising prototype needs a repository, CI/CD, secrets, sign-in, access control, a governed path to customer data, service accounts, routing, monitoring, and someone who owns it in production. Each of those is a ticket, an approval, or a configuration file someone has to get exactly right. Most teams building their first AI app had never done any of it.

The failures were silent

The worst part wasn't the amount of work. It was that many steps fail quietly. A platform default that is switched off. Configuration that lives in a separate repository nobody mentioned. An infrastructure change that was written but never applied. A DNS record that doesn't exist yet. An access group that nobody added the users to. Each one is easy to fix once you know about it, and every new team was rediscovering them.

Two paths, written down

So I wrote it down as a launch playbook with two clear paths. If you're testing an idea, move quickly on a constrained experimentation platform and don't pay for production yet. If the app needs durable data, sensitive integrations, or broad adoption, take the full production route, and here is every step, in order, including the failure modes I verified while shipping real apps.

One pattern from that work I now use everywhere: deploy thin first. Get a minimal service building, passing security gates, and answering a health check before you add databases, authentication, and live data. When something breaks, you know it's the deployment and not your app.

Then make the path disappear

A playbook helps people follow the path. The better move is to remove most of it. That's why I now own an internal AI application platform that handles sign-in, access, governed data and PII access, LLM access, and deployment once, at the platform level. A new app becomes a folder and a config file instead of weeks of tickets.

Good enablement is not doing every project for people. It is creating a path they can follow without rediscovering every hidden failure themselves.
